# 02 — Market Demand

**Researched:** 2026-07-31. All queries run live via web search unless marked otherwise.
**Critical limitation stated up front:** no keyword volume, difficulty or CPC data was obtainable from any accessible source in this phase. Nothing in this file is a search-volume claim. Demand is characterised through *market structure, competitor density, pricing behaviour and published industry statistics* — each with its provenance and confidence.

## 1. The category has consolidated — and that is the central finding

Eighteen months ago "AI receptionist" was an emerging term. As of mid-2026 it is a **defined product category with an established price band, comparison journalism, and venture funding**.

**Evidence of category maturity:**

- A dense layer of comparison content now exists ("Best AI Receptionists for Small Business 2026", "AI Receptionist Cost 2026", "10 Best AI Answering Services Tested & Ranked"). Comparison journalism at this density is a reliable signal that a category has consolidated and that buyers are in *evaluation*, not *discovery*.
- Named competitors with public pricing: **Jobber AI Receptionist $99/mo**, **Goodcall $66–$208/mo**, **Numa $49/mo**, **Smith.ai $95–$300/mo (hybrid AI+human)**, **Rosie from $49/mo**, **NextPhone $199/mo**, **Dialzara**, **Upfirst**, **Marlie AI**, **Allo**, **Retell**, **Synthflow**, **My AI Front Desk**, **Avoca**, **Sameday**, **Newo**, **LeadTruffle**, **ServiceAgent**, **AnswerForce**.
- Reported consensus price band: **most small businesses pay $99–$249/month**; the "actually worth using" flat-rate band is cited as **$149–$199/month**.
- **VC/PE investment into voice-AI companies reached a reported $4.2B in 2025, ~2.3× 2023.** *(Confidence: Moderate — secondary source, methodology not published.)*

**Sources:** getaira.io, dolfyn.ai, getnextphone.com, oncallclerk.com, pipelineon.com, callbirdai.com, retellai.com, upfirst.ai, ringly.io, withallo.com, resonateapp.com. Retrieved 2026-07-31, US-region search.

### 1.1 The bundling event — the most strategically important fact in this report

The AI receptionist is **ceasing to be a product and becoming a feature of software contractors already pay for.**

| Platform | AI receptionist status | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Jobber** | AI Receptionist **add-on**; included free on the Plus plan | **$99/mo add-on**; Plus plan $499–599/mo |
| **Housecall Pro** | Ships **built-in** AI receptionist; CSR AI and Marketing AI included in higher tiers; HCP Assist live answering as add-on | Plans $59–$329/mo |
| **ServiceTitan** | No native AI receptionist; building "Titan Intelligence" layer | Enterprise |

*(Confidence: Strongly supported — consistent across multiple independent comparison sources. Confidence: Moderate on exact plan inclusions, which change frequently; verify against vendor pricing pages before publishing any comparison content.)*

**Why this decides strategy:** Jobber's Canadian install base among small trades is large, and Jobber is the most-recommended job-management platform for small Canadian contractors. A prospect who already pays Jobber can add an AI receptionist for **$99/month with one click and zero integration work**. Against that, a **$1,500 CAD one-time build that answers the phone** is a hard sell on the receptionist function alone — because the function is no longer scarce.

The build only wins where the platform add-on structurally cannot go: **custom logic, cross-tool integration, data the client owns, and a human who is accountable when it breaks.**

## 2. Demand for the underlying problem is real and well-evidenced

The *category* is commoditised. The *problem* is not solved. These are different things and the gap between them is where the opportunity is.

### 2.1 Missed calls

| Statistic | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **27%** of calls to home-services businesses go unanswered | **Invoca** — states "our research found"; original researcher; blog dated 2024-05-23. Sample size, methodology and data year **not disclosed** | **Moderate** — primary-ish but unmethodologised |
| **62%** missed-call rate | Attributed by many secondary blogs to a "2024 ServiceTitan study of 50,000+ contractor phone lines". **I could not locate the primary study.** | **Low — do not republish as fact** |
| 411 Locals: 85 businesses across 58 industries answered only **37.8%** of calls | Secondary citation | **Low-Moderate** — small sample, old |
| **85%** of missed callers never call back | Secondary, widely repeated, no primary located | **Low** |
| **78%** of customers buy from the first company that responds | Secondary, widely repeated | **Low-Moderate** |

**This matters for the site's own credibility.** The 62% figure is the one most commonly quoted in this space, and it is the least verifiable. A brand positioning on honesty should cite Invoca's 27% *with its limitations stated* and refuse the 62% — that refusal is itself a differentiator in a market where every competitor repeats it.

### 2.2 Speed to lead

- MIT / InsideSales: contacting a lead within 5 minutes → **21× more likely to qualify** vs. 30 minutes. *(Confidence: Moderate-High — the most durable and widely-replicated finding in the set, though the original study is old and the "21x" is frequently restated without its conditions.)*
- Velocify: contact within 1 minute → **+391% conversion**. *(Confidence: Low-Moderate.)*
- Reported average industry response time: **42 hours**; only **0.1%** of inbound leads engaged in under 5 minutes. *(Confidence: Low — no primary located.)*

### 2.3 Estimate and quote follow-up — the underserved demand

- "Roughly **80%** of deals need five or more follow-ups, yet most contractors stop after one." *(Confidence: Low-Moderate; classic sales-literature figure, provenance unclear.)*
- Homeowners "rarely say no; they go quiet, then hire whoever stayed in touch."
- A **10-point lift in unsold-estimate close rate** can add six figures annually for a mid-size company. *(Confidence: Low — vendor-published.)*

**Structural observation, high confidence:** the competitor density around *quote follow-up* is markedly thinner than around *call answering*. Nearly every voice-AI vendor sells "never miss a call". Far fewer sell "never lose a quote." The pain is equally real, the money per event is larger (a quote represents a qualified, site-visited lead), and the workflow is more custom — which is precisely where a build beats a $99 add-on.

### 2.4 After-hours and emergency

- "Emergency calls carry 2× premium pricing and **60% come after hours**." *(Confidence: Low-Moderate — vendor-published, no primary.)*
- After-hours triage/dispatch is a named feature across HVAC/plumbing voice vendors, which corroborates that buyers ask for it.

### 2.5 Contractor AI adoption — the timing signal

- **Only 25% of contractors use AI**, while **74% see AI as an efficiency engine**. *(Confidence: Low-Moderate — secondary.)*
- Early adopters report **48% productivity gains**. *(Confidence: Low.)*

If directionally right, this is a market in early-majority transition: high stated intent, low actual adoption. That gap is usually a *trust and implementation* gap, not an awareness gap — which favours a done-for-you installer over another self-serve tool.

### 2.6 The counter-signal — AI project failure

- **42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025**, up from 17% the prior year. *(Confidence: Moderate — widely cited, attributed to S&P Global.)*
- **MIT: 95% of GenAI projects fail to deliver significant value.** *(Confidence: Moderate — widely cited 2025 finding.)*
- **67% of businesses reported chatbot technology did not meet expectations** (2025 study). *(Confidence: Low-Moderate.)*

**Read this as an asset, not a threat.** A rising failure rate means the buyer's dominant emotion is now scepticism, not excitement. The winning message in a sceptical market is not "AI is amazing" — it is "here is exactly what it does, here is what it does not do, here is what happens when it fails, and here is a real recording." The current site's honest register ("Who this is — and isn't — for", "Why not just buy the cheap AI receptionist?") is already aligned with this. It just lacks evidence.

## 3. Subscription fatigue — supports the ownership position

- The average company with **fewer than 200 employees runs 42 SaaS applications**. *(Confidence: Moderate.)*
- True total cost of ownership of SaaS is typically **2.5×–4× the base subscription price** once implementation, integration and customisation are counted. *(Confidence: Low-Moderate — vendor-published.)*
- Custom build economics: "SaaS runs $50–500/user/month forever, while custom is $4K–150K once"; break-even commonly cited at **12–24 months**. *(Confidence: Low-Moderate.)*
- Agentic coding tools have **materially reduced build cost**, with routine development tasks down an estimated 30–50%. *(Confidence: Moderate — directionally consistent across sources; the specific percentage is soft.)*

**Strategic read:** the "own it, don't rent it" argument now has genuine tailwind — but it is a *reasoned* argument, not an emotional one, and it takes longer to land than "$99/month, live today." It works best when the buyer has already been burned once. That makes **switchers**, not first-timers, the highest-converting segment.

## 4. Market sizing

**United States** — home-services market **US$842.04B in 2026**, projected US$989.22B by 2031 (3.27% CAGR). California ~20% of US total; Texas second; Florida third. *(Source: Mordor Intelligence via search summary. Confidence: Moderate — commercial research firm, methodology paywalled.)*

**Canada / British Columbia** — **>28,000 construction companies in BC**, up 160+ since fall 2024. **Small firms (<20 employees) were 85% of BC construction businesses in 2022.** Construction-trades workers up 14% year-over-year. *(Source: BC Construction Association Stat Pack, Spring 2026; VRCA. Confidence: High — industry association primary data.)*

**The addressable reality:** the serviceable market for a solo builder is not "28,000 BC construction companies." At one build every 1–2 weeks, capacity is roughly **25–50 builds/year**. Market size is therefore **not the constraint** and should not drive strategy. **Proof, referral flow and delivery capacity are the constraints.** This is the strongest argument for going deep in one trade in one region rather than wide across 39 industries and 19 cities.
