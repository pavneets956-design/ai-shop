# Raw — Statistics and Their Provenance

**Date:** 2026-07-31

This file exists because the AI-receptionist content market runs on recycled statistics of poor provenance. Before any number from this research is published on the site, check it here.

## 🔴 DO NOT PUBLISH

### "62% of contractor calls go unanswered"

The single most-repeated statistic in this category. Attributed by numerous secondary blogs to **"a 2024 ServiceTitan study analyzing 50,000+ contractor phone lines."**

**I could not locate the primary study.** Every source citing it is a vendor blog citing another vendor blog. No ServiceTitan-published research document was found.

**Also appearing at:** contractorincharge.com ("62% Shocking Loss"), medium.com, calljolt.com, agentzap.ai, massmonopoly.com.

**Verdict: do not use.** For a brand positioning on honesty, publishing the least verifiable number in the market is the cheapest possible credibility loss — and refusing it is a differentiator, because everyone else repeats it.

## 🟡 PUBLISH WITH CAVEAT

### "27% of calls to home-services businesses are not answered" — Invoca

- **Invoca is the original researcher** — the article states "our research found."
- Blog dated **2024-05-23**.
- **Sample size, methodology and data year are NOT disclosed.**
- The article's revenue example assumes a $4,500 furnace replacement at 20% margin ($900/job) — it does **not** establish a general per-call value.

**Recommended framing:**
> "Invoca's own research puts it at 27% of calls to home-services businesses going unanswered. Invoca doesn't publish the sample size, methodology or data year, so treat it as indicative rather than definitive."

### "21× more likely to qualify a lead contacted within 5 minutes vs 30 minutes"

MIT / InsideSales / Lead Response Management Study. The most durable and widely replicated finding in the set — but the original study is old and the figure is usually restated without its original conditions. **Attribute it and don't over-claim.**

### "29% of consumers would hang up if forced to speak to AI"

Vendor-published, but **more useful than the vendor counter-claim** because it describes the buyer's felt risk rather than the vendor's preferred reality. Attribute it.

### AI project failure rates

- **42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025** (up from 17%) — widely cited, attributed to S&P Global. **Moderate confidence.**
- **MIT: 95% of GenAI projects fail to deliver significant value** — widely cited 2025 finding. **Moderate confidence.**

Both support the scepticism thesis and are safe to cite with attribution.

## 🟢 SAFE TO PUBLISH

### BC Construction Association (Spring 2026 Stat Pack)

- **>28,000 construction companies in BC**, up 160+ since fall 2024
- **Small firms (<20 employees) = 85% of BC construction businesses** (2022)
- SMEs = **over 70%** of construction employment
- Construction-trades workers **+14% year-over-year**

Industry-association primary data. **Highest confidence in this research.**

### Regulatory facts

- **PIPEDA** requires all-parties consent for commercial call recording — inform, state purpose, seek consent; continuing implies consent. *(Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada — primary regulator.)*
- **Bill C-27 died on prorogation, 2025-01-06.** No federal AI framework in force.
- **CASL**: express consent doesn't expire; implied consent valid only if the last transaction was within **2 years**; sender ID and unsubscribe required; penalties to **CA$10M** per violation.

Safe to publish **with a "not legal advice / verify with counsel" caveat.**

## The opportunity hiding in this file

The category's data layer is weak. **A study of even 50 Fraser Valley trade businesses** — real answer rates, real callback times, real quote-close rates, methodology published — would be **the best-sourced dataset in this market.**

It is simultaneously the only realistic route to domain authority and a legitimate reason to call fifty local trade businesses. That is not a coincidence worth wasting.
