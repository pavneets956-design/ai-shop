# 00 — Executive Verdict

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Market:** Canada (primary), US (context) · **Collector:** Surrey, BC

# `PARTIAL EVIDENCE — PROCEED WITH LISTED ASSUMPTIONS`

Live evidence was obtained and is substantial: **2,400 autocomplete requests** (100% success, 0 throttle events, 8,822 suggestions), **34 usable live Canadian SERPs** with People Also Ask, and **334 Reddit posts** across seven contractor subreddits.

It is *partial* for four specific reasons, none of which is recoverable in this environment:

1. **No search-volume, CPC or difficulty data exists in this study.** No keyword tool is available here. Every such field in `07-keyword-intent-map.tsv` is literally `UNKNOWN`. Nothing was estimated.
2. **PAA depth is level 1 only.** Levels 2–3 need click-expansion on a live page; the IP was throttled before that was possible.
3. **14 SERP collections failed** — including five *local* queries, which is the most valuable gap, since local produced the only AI-Overview-free results found.
4. **No fresh GSC export, no G2/Capterra mining, no Google Trends.** All `[U]`.

Proceed on the assumptions listed in §11 below. Do not treat the directional signals as volume.

## The ten answers

### 1. What are contractors actually searching for?
Problems, not categories. The three largest measured vocabulary clusters are **`missed call` (347 variants)**, **`receptionist` (333)** and **`answering service` (270)**. By contrast **`ai automation` has 14**. They search for the symptom and the familiar product category — never for "AI automation for contractors".

### 2. Which problem has the strongest commercial intent?
**Cost of an AI receptionist.** `how much does an ai receptionist cost` is the standout: no ads, cost-led PAA (*"Is an AI receptionist worth it?"*), and three Canadian competitors already ranking. It is a buying question in a category where, per `06`, nobody publishes plain prices — and this business already has an approved CAD range in a canonical registry.

Runner-up: **quote follow-up**. *"How to professionally follow up on an estimate?"* is the single most repeated question in the whole study — it appears in **three separate PAA trees** — and Reddit, Facebook and Quora all rank on those SERPs, meaning existing answers aren't satisfying anyone.

### 3. Which customer segment shows the best opportunity?
**Owner-operator to ~15 employees, Metro Vancouver / Fraser Valley.** `[D]` from Reddit composition (*"Is Jobber worth it for small teams?"*, *"Starting a roofing company what CRM do you use?"*) and from local SERPs being the only AI-Overview-free ones found. This matches the existing $1,500–$7,500 CAD project pricing.

### 4. Is "AI automation for contractors" the right category language?
**No.** 14 autocomplete variants. It is how the business describes itself. Use problem language (`quotes go quiet`, `missed calls`) and familiar product language (`AI receptionist`, `answering service`) instead.

### 5. Is "Quote Rescue" supported by demand, or internal branding?
**Internal branding.** Zero occurrences across 3,660 CA suggestions, all collected PAA, and 334 Reddit titles. Keep it as a product name for sales conversations; never use it as an H1, a nav label, or an SEO target.

### 6. Which five pages should be the primary acquisition pages?
1. `/locations/surrey` — targets the only two AI-Overview-free SERPs found
2. `/pricing` — targets the strongest commercial query
3. `/tools` + the calculators — the one asset class no competitor on 34 SERPs shipped
4. `/industries/roofing` — the open trade-specificity gap
5. `/ai-business-system` — targets `contractor software without subscription`

### 7. Which five free tools should be prioritised?
Three already ship and need retargeting, not rebuilding: **missed-call revenue calculator**, **quote follow-up generator**, **lead-leak audit**. Two to build: **quote-recovery value calculator** and **AI receptionist cost calculator**. Full scoring in `08`.

### 8. Which current pages are unsupported by demand?
Anything targeting `contractor office automation` (Google reads it as industrial controls), `contractor review requests` (homeowner intent), `ai consultant for contractors` (bdc.ca owns it), `done for you business automation` (zero contractor and zero Canadian results), and `roofing estimating software` (roofr/roofsnap own it; not a product this business sells). **Flagged only — no noindex or consolidation is authorized in this branch.**

### 9. What should change in the proposed homepage?
- H1 → **"Quotes go quiet. Calls get missed. We install the system that catches them."**
- Nav "Get a Fit Check" → **"See what it's costing you"** ("fit check" has zero search presence)
- Nav "Tools" → **"Free Tools"**
- Problem selector → *"I'm missing calls while I'm on site"*, *"I'm doing admin at 9pm"*
- Add an explicit objection block — the four verified fears, answered in customer words
- Do **not** lead with "Done-for-you AI systems" or "Canadian"

### 10. What should not change?
The free tools (strongest asset), the pricing registry as source of truth, the 216-page architecture, both receptionist money pages, the location pages, and the refusal to fabricate proof.

## Two findings that should change the plan

**A. The competition is not who the brief assumed.** Across 34 live SERPs, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Podium, Rosie, Goodcall and Avoca appear **zero** times; Jobber appears once. The actual page-one occupants are Reddit (30/34), Canadian software directories (`capterra.ca`, `getapp.ca`), and **roughly a dozen small BC/Canadian AI agencies** — `adaptai.ca`, `automatebc.ca`, `manndigital.ca`, `buildgravity.ca`, `futureforgeaisolutions.ca`, `mihronai.ca`, `voxara.ca`, `askbenny.ca`, and others. `adaptai.ca` ranks on all three local queries. These are the real competitors.

**B. "We're Canadian" is not a differentiator.** Jobber is a Canadian company (Edmonton) with heavy Canadian brand-navigation search. Google's own PAA asks *"Is Jobber a Canadian company?"*. **"Surrey"** is defensible as a specific local claim; "Canadian" is table stakes.

## §11 — Assumptions to proceed under

1. Autocomplete breadth and PAA recurrence stand in for volume. They are ordinal, not cardinal.
2. Local demand is inferred from two AI-Overview-free SERPs; **five further local queries failed to collect** and should be re-run before committing budget.
3. The 1–15 employee segment is inferred from discussion composition, not firmographics.
4. Competitor pricing opacity is inferred from SERP composition — competitor pages were not fetched.
5. Collector is physically in Surrey; local results are "what a Surrey searcher sees", not a national view.
6. AI Overview presence was detected by string match on rendered SERP text; it is a presence flag, not a measure of click suppression.
