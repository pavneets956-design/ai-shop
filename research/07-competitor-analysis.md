## 1. The competitive set is four layers deep, not one

Handbuilt is not competing with "other AI agencies." It is competing simultaneously with four categories that each answer the same buyer question — *how do I stop losing jobs to the phone?* — at four different price points.

### Layer 1 — Platform-bundled AI (the existential threat)

| Vendor | Offer | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Jobber** | AI Receptionist add-on; free on Plus | **$99/mo** (Plus $499–599/mo) |
| **Housecall Pro** | Built-in AI receptionist; CSR AI + Marketing AI in higher tiers; HCP Assist add-on | Plans **$59–$329/mo** |
| **ServiceTitan** | No native AI receptionist; "Titan Intelligence" layer in development | Enterprise |

**Why this is the threat that matters.** The target buyer *already pays one of these*. Jobber is the most-recommended job-management platform for small Canadian contractors, at $49–$499 CAD/mo. For that buyer, adding an AI receptionist is a checkbox and $99 — no integration, no vendor, no risk, no onboarding.

**What it cannot do:** it is confined to the platform. It cannot reach into a second system, cannot implement custom quoting logic, cannot be modified beyond its settings, and the client owns none of it. **Every honest differentiation argument Handbuilt has lives in that sentence.**

**Implication:** any Handbuilt page that argues "you should have an AI receptionist" is doing Jobber's marketing. Pages must argue the *next* question: *you have one — why is it still not booking your jobs properly?*

### Layer 2 — Standalone AI receptionist SaaS (the price anchor)

**Numa $49/mo · Rosie from $49/mo · Goodcall $66–$208/mo · NextPhone $199/mo · Smith.ai $95–$300/mo (hybrid) · Dialzara · Upfirst · Marlie AI · Allo · My AI Front Desk · Retell · Synthflow · Avoca · Sameday · Newo · LeadTruffle · ServiceAgent · Xenara**

Consensus band: **$99–$249/mo**, with **$149–$199 flat-rate** cited as the sensible middle.

Documented pricing traps — useful, because they are the market's soft underbelly:
- Dialzara **$0.48/min** overages
- AnswerForce **$2.00/min** overages
- Rosie's $49 tier **excludes appointment booking**
- Undisclosed setup fees **$75–$1,500+**

Trade focus is explicit and concentrated: **HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing** (Avoca, Sameday, AnswerForce, Newo, Xenara, LeadTruffle all name these).

### Layer 3 — Human and hybrid answering services (the incumbent being replaced)

| Vendor | Rating | Documented weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Ruby** | G2 **3.8/5**; Trustpilot 4.6/5 (834 reviews) | Most expensive. One Capterra reviewer's **$660 expected invoice became $5,100+ with no warning** |
| **Smith.ai** | AI Receptionist G2 **4.7/5**; human VR **4.9/5**; Trustpilot **4.3/5** (336 reviews) | Trustpilot reports **AI auto-transferring to live agents without authorization**, inflating bills |
| **AnswerForce, ReceptionHQ, AnswerFirst, Absent Answer, PATLive** | — | Per-minute/per-call overage exposure |

Market pricing: **$135–$450/mo** typical for small business; **$0.75–$1.50 per call** or **$0.90–$1.50 per minute**. A contractor forum poster reported paying **$80/month** and being happy with it.

**The recurring complaint pattern across Trustpilot, G2, Clutch and BBB is remarkably consistent:** (1) billing surprises from stacked add-ons, (2) unauthorised transfers to live agents, (3) charges continuing after cancellation.

**This is the most exploitable fact in the entire competitive landscape.** The dominant emotion in this market is not "I need AI" — it is **"I don't trust what this will cost me next month."** A fixed one-time price answers that better than any feature.

## 2. Competitor content matrix — where the gaps actually are

| Topic | Competitor coverage | Quality of coverage | Gap for Handbuilt |
|---|---|---|---|
| "AI receptionist cost" | Saturated | Good — detailed, honest about traps | **None. Do not compete.** |
| "Best AI receptionist" listicles | Saturated | Mostly vendor-authored, self-favouring | Low — you would be one more vendor listicle |
| AI receptionist vs answering service | Saturated | Good | Low |
| **Estimate / quote follow-up systems** | **Thin** | Mostly generic advice posts | **HIGH — best content gap found** |
| **"I already have Jobber's AI receptionist — why isn't it working?"** | **Effectively zero** | — | **HIGHEST. Nobody writes this because every vendor sells the thing.** |
| **Ownership / what happens when you leave** | **Thin** — appears in buyer-checklist articles, almost never on vendor sites | Vendors avoid it | **HIGH — structural gap; competitors cannot fill it without indicting themselves** |
| **Failure handling / escalation, honestly** | Thin | Vendors claim it, few demonstrate it | **HIGH** |
| **Real recorded calls, unedited** | **Very thin** | Vendors show curated demos | **HIGHEST-VALUE PROOF, lowest supply** |
| Canadian compliance (PIPEDA / Loi 25 / CASL) | **Now covered** by Canadian-native vendors | Adequate | **Closed — was an opportunity, no longer** |
| Integration matrix by platform | Moderate | Generic | Moderate |
| Trade-specific call scripts | Thin | — | Moderate-High |

**The pattern:** every gap that remains is a gap **competitors cannot fill without damaging themselves.** A SaaS vendor cannot honestly write "here's why the AI receptionist you're renting won't fix this," because they are renting you one. A one-person studio selling owned builds can. That asymmetry — not keywords — is the content strategy.

## 3. Competitive position of Handbuilt, stated bluntly

| Dimension | Handbuilt | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Price | **$1,500 CAD one-time** + optional $99/mo care | vs. $99/mo bundled — **higher upfront, cheaper at ~15 months.** The argument is real but requires a patient buyer |
| Setup time | ~5 business days | vs. same-day for SaaS — **loses on speed** |
| Ownership | **Client owns code and accounts** | **Wins. Uniquely and structurally.** |
| Integration depth | Custom, cross-tool | **Wins vs. platform-bundled** |
| Accountability | One named builder | **Wins** — no support queue |
| Proof | **None** | **Loses to everyone, including the $49/mo vendors who have G2 ratings** |
| Entity presence | **None** | **Loses to every competitor including local ones** |
| Compliance positioning | Not stated on site | **Loses to Mihron, Voxify, Dialbox** |
| Trade specialisation | Claims 39 industries | **Loses to Avoca/Sameday**, who claim 3–4 and are believed |

**The two lines that decide this:** proof and entity. Handbuilt's *offer* is genuinely differentiated — ownership plus accountability is real and the market demonstrably asks for it. Its *credibility* is currently zero, and in a market where 42% of AI projects get abandoned, credibility is the whole purchase decision.

A $49/mo competitor with 200 G2 reviews beats a $1,500 build with none, regardless of which is technically better.

## 4. Unoccupied positioning — the one gap worth taking

Mapping the market on two axes:

```
                    OWNED  ▲
                           │
                           │        ◄── EMPTY. This is the position.
                           │            (owned + installed for you)
                           │
  DIY ◄──────────────────  ┼  ──────────────────► DONE FOR YOU
  Zapier, Make,            │            Jobber AI, Rosie, Smith.ai,
  n8n, GoHighLevel         │            Ruby, AnswerForce, Mihron
                           │
                           │
                   RENTED  ▼
```

- **Rented + done-for-you** — brutally crowded, price-anchored at $99–$249/mo, and consolidating into platforms.
- **Owned + DIY** — Zapier/Make/n8n. Cheap, but the contractor will never build it.
- **Owned + done-for-you** — nearly empty at the small-business price point. Custom-software agencies live here but start at $25k+ and do not talk to a 6-person fencing company.

**Handbuilt's defensible position: the only person who will build a $1,500–$7,500 owned system for a trades business and put their name on it.**

The risk to name honestly: this position is defensible but **small and slow**. It requires an educated buyer who has already been burned. It will never out-convert "$99/mo, live today" on a cold visitor. It converts *switchers*. That is a smaller pool — and it means outreach, referral and proof matter far more than SEO volume. Which is consistent with everything else in this research.

## 5. What not to do

- **Do not copy competitor wording or design.** The listicle-and-comparison layer is saturated with near-identical vendor-authored content; entering it adds nothing and dilutes the entity further.
- **Do not compete on the "AI receptionist cost" keyword.** Well-resourced vendors with real data own it and are, on the evidence, covering it honestly.
- **Do not claim compliance credentials that do not exist.** Mihron cites PIPEDA and PHIPA; Voxify cites Loi 25. Matching that language without matching the underlying practice is exactly the kind of unsupported claim this research is required to prevent.
