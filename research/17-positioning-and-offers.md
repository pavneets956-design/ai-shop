# 17 — Positioning and Offers

**Researched:** 2026-07-31

---

## 1. The strategic problem, stated once

Handbuilt currently leads with **"AI receptionist,"** priced at **$1,500 CAD one-time**.

Eighteen months ago that was a differentiated offer. As of mid-2026 it is not, for one reason: **the AI receptionist has stopped being a product and become a feature of software the buyer already pays for.** Jobber ships it as a $99/mo add-on (free on Plus). Housecall Pro ships it built in. Standalone vendors have anchored the price at $99–$249/mo, with entrants at $14–$49.

A prospect on Jobber can have an AI receptionist for $99/month, today, with zero integration work. Against that, "a $1,500 one-time build that answers your phone" loses — not because it is worse, but because **the function is no longer scarce.**

Leading with "AI receptionist" therefore enters a price comparison that cannot be won. The positioning must move to ground where the comparison is different.

## 3. Recommended positioning

**Primary:**
> **The AI office system you own — built for your trade.**

**Secondary explanation:**
> Your phone gets answered, your quotes get followed up, and your invoices get chased — by a system built for how *you* work. One fixed price. You own the code and the accounts. Built and supported by one person in Surrey, BC.

**Core promise:**
> Stop losing jobs to missed calls and quotes that go quiet — without renting another subscription you'll never own.

**Primary audience:** owner-operators of field-service trades, 2–15 staff, roughly $300K–$2M revenue, already paying for Jobber or Housecall Pro. **Highest-converting sub-segment: switchers** — those who already tried a $99/mo tool and found it insufficient.

**Main CTA:** *Book a fit check* — keep it. Low-commitment, honest, and consistent with a positioning built on not overselling.

**Entry offer:** the **free tools** — genuinely useful, no signup, no email wall. They are already the best lead magnets on the site; they just need to be linked and promoted.

**Higher-value offer:** the connected system — $3,500–$7,500 CAD, 2–4 workers, one integrated whole. **This should be the flagship, not the receptionist.** It is the tier where "you own it" is worth paying for and where a platform add-on genuinely cannot compete.

## 4. The pricing decision

This is the sharpest strategic choice in the report.

**Problem:** $1,500 for a receptionist invites a direct comparison with $99/mo, and at that comparison the buyer computes a 15-month payback for something they can have today for $99. The number is defensible but the *frame* is losing.

**Three options:**

| Option | Argument | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Hold $1,500, change the frame** | Stop selling "a receptionist." Sell "your phone + your quotes + your CRM, connected and owned." Different object, no direct comparison. | Requires discipline in every piece of copy |
| **B. Raise the entry to $2,500+ and drop the standalone receptionist** | Removes the losing comparison entirely; positions as a system builder only | Smaller top of funnel; harder first sale with no proof |
| **C. Add a low-price diagnostic entry** ($199–$499 lead-leak audit → credited to a build) | Qualifies buyers, produces the first-party data needed for the benchmark study, converts the free tools into revenue | Adds a step; risks looking like a consulting upsell |

**Recommendation: A now, C within a quarter, revisit B once three case studies exist.**

Rationale: the $1,500 entry is the only thing generating first conversations while proof is zero, so removing it now (B) is premature. Reframing (A) costs nothing but copy discipline. C is genuinely attractive because the paid audit **produces the first-party benchmark data** that Tier-3 authority content needs — it turns a sales step into a research asset.

**The one pricing change that must happen immediately, regardless:** resolve the three-way contradiction on `/ai-business-system` (`01-current-site-audit.md` §3.1). A prospect who sees $2,500–5,000 in the copy, $3,500–5,000 "typically $7,500" in a cross-reference, and $3,500–$7,500 in the schema does not conclude "flexible pricing." They conclude the business is disorganised — on a page selling operational competence.

## 5. Trust and proof strategy

The offer is sound. **Credibility is zero, and in a market where 42% of AI projects were abandoned in 2025, credibility is the entire purchase decision.** A $49/mo competitor with 200 G2 reviews beats a $1,500 build with none.

**Proof, in priority order:**

| # | Asset | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One real recorded call**, consented | Answers the two severest objections at once. Almost no competitor publishes one | One client |
| 2 | **One named client + one real number** | Converts every claim on the site from assertion to fact | One client |
| 3 | **Live demo line** the visitor can phone now | Removes "does it sound robotic?" instantly, at zero marginal cost per visitor | Build time |
| 4 | Integration screenshot — booking landing in Jobber | Proves the differentiator rather than claiming it | One client |
| 5 | Founder identity — full name, face, `Person` schema | Strongest E-E-A-T lever a solo studio has | Free |
| 6 | Original benchmark study | Only route to real domain authority | Weeks |

**How to buy #1 and #2:** offer the next 2–3 builds at a significant discount **explicitly in exchange for** a named case study, a recorded call and a reference call. Price the discount as marketing spend, because that is what it is. This is the fastest legitimate path from zero proof to sufficient proof, and it is the only recommendation in this report that directly unblocks every other one.

## 6. Language to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Never miss another call" as an absolute | Disproved on call three. Say "catch the ones you're losing now" |
| "Trusted by contractors across BC" | **Unsupportable today.** Do not write it until it is true |
| The 62% missed-call statistic | Unverifiable primary source. Using it forfeits the honesty position for a number competitors already overuse |
| "Enterprise-grade", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "revolutionary" | Sceptical market; these read as the language of the vendors that burned them |
| "AI-powered" as the lead | The buyer wants the outcome; AI is the mechanism, never the desire |
| Any customer count, ROI or revenue figure not measured | Permanent liability once ingested by an AI engine |
| PIPEDA/PHIPA/Loi 25 compliance claims | Only if the underlying practice actually exists |
| "39 industries" | Reads as "no specialisation." Name 4–6 |

## 7. The audience fork — a decision that must be made

The site currently sells to **contractors** and **content creators** (20 `/creators` pages).

Carrying both is the one option clearly worse than either choice made deliberately:
- **Entity dilution** — clear service definition and explicit target industries are the top-ranked GEO factors. "We serve contractors and TikTok creators" answers *"what is this company?"* badly.
- **Doubled proof burden** — zero proof in two verticals, same founder-hours.
- **No shared anything** — vocabulary, buying trigger, price expectation, referral path. A fencing contractor never refers a YouTuber.
- **Worse fight** — creator tooling competes with $20/mo self-serve products and fast-moving open source.

**Recommendation: `noindex` the 20 `/creators` pages and focus entirely on trades**, or spin creators to a separate domain with its own entity. Reversible either way; `noindex` preserves the work.

This is the founder's call, and it is a real cost — that vertical represents genuine shipped work. But the research is unambiguous that the *combination* is what damages both.
