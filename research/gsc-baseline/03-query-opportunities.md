## 3. Queries ranking 4–15 — the classic "quick win" band

**There are none.**

The best non-branded query position in the entire dataset is **8.5** (`what is the best way to automate customer support replies with ai while keeping accurate order details?`, 2 impressions) and **9.0** (an Intercom/Fin site-exclusion operator query, 1 impression). Both are noise — one is a long conversational question, the other is somebody running a competitor-research search with `-site:` operators.

**Implication:** the standard SEO playbook for this stage — find queries at positions 4–15 and push them onto page 1 — **does not apply here.** There is nothing in striking distance. This site is not one optimisation pass away from traffic.

## 4. High impressions, poor CTR

**Not applicable in the normal sense.** Every page with meaningful impressions sits at position 68–73, where CTR is expected to be ~0%. `/ai-receptionist` (146 impressions, 0 clicks, position 72.5) is not a CTR problem — it is a ranking problem. Rewriting its title tag would change nothing.

The only two pages where CTR is even measurable — `/` and `/industries` — are both already at **5.0%**, which is healthy.

### Quote-heavy trades (fencing, decks, concrete) — **NOT YET REPRESENTED**

**Zero queries** mention fencing, decks or concrete. The site has industry pages for `fence-company` and `deck-builder`, and neither received a single impression.

Per the assignment's own rule, **this is not evidence of absent demand** — the pages exist but at position ~64 average the site has no visibility for anything, and these pages are among the 137 with no data at all.

**Verdict: untested, not disproven.**

### What IS actually pulling impressions

The two biggest clusters are **AI receptionist (174)** and **chatbot (105)** — 63.8% combined. Phase 1 recommended de-emphasising both as commoditised.

**The tension, stated honestly:** the measured data says the receptionist and chatbot clusters are the only things generating meaningful impressions. The strategic analysis says those are exactly the categories being commoditised by Jobber and Housecall Pro. Both can be true — the site attracts what it has built, and what it has built is in a market it cannot win on price. **The measured data does not resolve the strategy question; it confirms the site currently has no position in either direction.**

- **Long conversational queries** appear repeatedly (`can ai follow up with old leads without my team touching them`, `what is the best way to automate customer support replies…`). Consistent with AI-assistant-style search behaviour and supports the AEO/GEO emphasis in Phase 1.

## 7. What to do with this

**Not:** optimise titles, chase positions 4–15, or rewrite meta descriptions. There is nothing in range and no CTR problem.

**Instead:** treat the query list as a **cheap validation instrument, not a traffic source.** It tells you which language reaches you unassisted — `ai receptionist for contractors`, `ai leads for contractors`, `ai sales follow up system for construction company`, `contractor automation tools` — and that language should inform outreach scripts and the design-partner pitch in `08-design-partner-plan.md` far more than it should inform page production.

At 13 impressions/day, SEO is not currently a customer-acquisition channel for this business, and will not become one before the proof problem is solved.
