## 2. Why the US is Tier 2 despite being ~10× bigger

The binding constraint is **proof, not addressable market**. A solo builder has capacity for perhaps 25–50 builds per year — market size is not what limits revenue.

Against that constraint the US is *harder*, not easier:

| Factor | Canada (Metro Vancouver) | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Proof acquisition | Can meet the client, record a real call, get a named reference | Remote-only from day one |
| Competitive density | High but thinner locally | **Highest** — every funded voice-AI vendor is US-first |
| Local search leverage | GBP + service-area viable | Not viable without a US presence |
| Legal complexity | One federal regime (PIPEDA/CASL) | **Fifty state regimes** for call recording |
| Currency / invoicing | Native CAD | USD conversion, tax questions |
| Trust at the sale | "I'm in Surrey, I'll come by" | "I'm in another country" |

The US requires the thing Handbuilt most lacks — **credibility at a distance** — and offers no compensating advantage until that credibility exists.

## 3. The genuine US-specific complication: call-recording law

Canada is one federal regime. The US is **not**, and this is a real product and copy constraint, not a footnote.

- The US splits into **one-party consent** and **all-party (two-party) consent** states.
- California, Florida, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Washington and several others require **all-party consent**; the majority are one-party.
- **Federal law (18 U.S.C. §2511) sets a one-party floor; states may be stricter.** Interstate calls raise the question of which state's rule governs — commonly answered by applying the stricter.

**Consequence:** any AI receptionist that records or transcribes must handle consent **per state**. A single announcement script cannot be correct nationwide.

**This is also an opportunity.** A genuinely accurate, trade-specific, state-by-state consent guide is *information gain* — real research value, real link potential, and directly serving Root 5 of the question graph. It is one of the few US-facing assets worth building before US clients exist.

> **Do not publish state-by-state legal specifics without verifying each state against primary statute or a qualified source.** Nothing in this file is legal advice, and the state list above is indicative, not a verified enumeration.

## 4. Spanish-language track — researched and rejected

The US Hispanic contractor and homeowner population is large and materially underserved in this category. Bilingual EN/ES call handling is a genuine unmet need.

**Rejected for now** on capability, not opportunity: no Spanish-language delivery, support or QA capacity exists. Publishing Spanish landing pages for a service that cannot be delivered in Spanish would be an unsupportable claim — precisely the failure mode this research is meant to prevent.

Revisit only if a Spanish-speaking delivery partner is secured. **Watchlist, not backlog.**

## 6. Terminology differences to respect

| Concept | US | Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Trade business | "home services", "contractor" | "trades", "contractor" |
| Money | USD | **CAD — state it explicitly**; the site already does this well |
| Sales tax | sales tax | GST/PST/HST |
| Recording law | **state-dependent** | federal PIPEDA, all-parties |
| Anti-spam | TCPA | **CASL** (materially stricter) |
| Labour cost framing | "burden rate" | same |

The site's existing practice of stating "CAD" on every price is correct and should be preserved — it prevents a US visitor mis-reading $1,500 CAD as USD, which currently *understates* the price to a US buyer by roughly 25–30%.
