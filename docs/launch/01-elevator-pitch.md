# Elevator pitch

> **Draft. Not sent, not published.** Facts sourced from `research/transformation-2026-08-30/09-proof-founder-positioning.md`.

## The paragraph

I run a cedar-fence company in South Surrey, and I got tired of losing work because I was on a job site and couldn't answer the phone. So I built the system that answers it — trained on my services, my prices, my hours — and put it on my own line before I ever offered it to anyone else. That's what I do now: I build the AI office system for a trades business and hand it over. Your phone gets answered, your quotes get followed up, your invoices get chased. One fixed quote in Canadian dollars before any work starts, one person building it, and you own what's built. Starter systems begin at $1,500; a connected multi-worker system runs $3,500 to $7,500. I'm in Surrey, I've got no clients yet, and I'll tell you exactly what it won't do before you pay me anything.

## Notes on why each sentence is there

- **Opens with the fence company, not the software.** It is the one thing every contractor he talks to recognises immediately, and it is true and verifiable (`ironwoodgrounds.ca`, "Pavneet Singh — Founder & Builder").
- **"Put it on my own line before I ever offered it to anyone else"** is literally what happened — the receptionist ran on the real Ironwood DID in July–August 2026. It replaces a testimonial he does not have.
- **Prices are stated out loud** and match `lib/data/packages.ts`. Naming the number early filters out the people who want a $99/mo subscription, which is the wedge: owned + done-for-you, not rented.
- **"I've got no clients yet"** is the line that will make him wince. Keep it. It is going to come out in the first five minutes of any real conversation, and saying it first converts a weakness into the reason to believe everything else he says. If he wants a softer form: *"You'd be my first paying client for this, and I'm pricing it that way."*
- **"I'll tell you exactly what it won't do"** is the one differentiator he can demonstrate on the spot — the receptionist's business brain lists services explicitly *not* offered.
