9. What screenshots cannot show

  • Scroll reveal. Sections fade up 14px over 420ms once, via IntersectionObserver. Fully disabled under prefers-reduced-motion, and content renders visible if JS never runs.
  • Sticky process panel. On desktop the left card pins at top: nav + 32px while the three step cards scroll past. Disabled below 1024px — verified: the only sticky element at 390px is the header.
  • Live calculator. Three sliders recompute on every input; the output is aria-live="polite" so screen readers hear the change.
  • Problem selector. Real tabs — role="tab", aria-selected, aria-controls; the window-chrome label updates with the active tab.
  • FAQ accordion. Eight aria-expanded buttons, 44px toggles, one open at a time.
  • Mobile menu. Opens, locks body scroll, closes on Escape and restores scroll. Six 48px rows.
  • Focus rings. Every interactive element shows a red ring on keyboard focus. Verified on 40 elements.
  • Hover. Cards lift 2px with a deeper shadow; the peg icons and arrows shift on hover.

10. My own critique of this work

Strongest decision

Putting a working calculator where the template puts a customer-logo strip. It converts the biggest weakness — zero customers — into the one thing a brochure competitor cannot copy in an afternoon, and it does it above the fold. Across 34 collected SERPs no competitor shipped a working tool.

Weakest section

The final CTA band. Three cards in a 519px dark band leaves visible dead space beneath them, and the three options are not differentiated enough visually — “Book a review” is the one that matters and it only gets a white fill. The band reads as filler rather than a close.

Still template-like

The four system cards. Icon → title → three labelled rows → arrow link is the most generic pattern on the page; swap the copy and it could be any SaaS site. The pegboard icons are the only thing rescuing it.

The footer was never redesigned. I added links to it but left the old styling — smaller type, different muted grey, different spacing scale. It is visibly from the previous design system and it is the last thing on the page.

Copied too closely from Verseo

The bracketed eyebrow [ like this ] is lifted essentially verbatim — it is the template's single most recognisable typographic tell, and I reproduced it with only a red dot added. If Verseo is a template you have seen, you will recognise it. Everything else was translated (pegboard for dot-matrix, drafting sheet for sky photo, one accent instead of three pastels); this one was not, and it is the fair criticism to make.

The window chrome is also close, though it at least carries an argument here — “this runs inside your own accounts”.

Does it read as a premium contractor-AI company, or a generic AI agency?

Mostly the former, and the copy is doing more of that work than the design. “On the number you already own”, “while you’re on site”, “Cloverdale”, “gutter replacement estimate”, CAD prices on the page and the Surrey section are specific in a way a generic agency site is not. The restraint helps — no gradients, no glow, no stock imagery.

Where it slips: the layout system itself is correct rather than distinctive. A very well-executed premium SaaS page. Nothing about the grid, the cards or the type says trades. A contractor would find it credible and calm; they would not find it theirs.

What one more design pass would change

  1. Restyle the footer into the new system. It is the most visible inconsistency and it is cheap to fix.
  2. Rebuild the final CTA — kill the dead space, make “Book a review” dominant, demote the other two to text links.
  3. Replace the bracketed eyebrow with something of our own so the strongest borrowed element stops being borrowed.
  4. Give the four system cards a real visual — a small diagram per card instead of icon-plus-text.
  5. Push one genuinely trade-specific visual device through the page. The drafting sheet appears once, behind the process section; it should be the spine of the whole design rather than a single texture.
